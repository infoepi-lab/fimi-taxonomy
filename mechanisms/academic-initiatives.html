FIMI Taxonomy v4.1 — Modular taxonomy of foreign information manipulation and interference

Academic Initiatives

Legitimation
Malign academic initiatives are state-funded institutions that simulate scholarship while producing ideologically predetermined conclusions.
Author
Affiliation

E. Rosalie Li

Information Epidemiology Lab

Published

February 24, 2026

Malign academic initiatives are state-funded institutions that simulate scholarship while producing ideologically predetermined conclusions. Unlike intellectual capture, which co-opts external experts, these initiatives originate within the regime itself. Their function is to convert propaganda into research—to clothe ideology in academic form.

Soviet precedent is instructive. The Institute for the Study of the USA and Canada produced Cold War “research” framing racial inequality and capitalism as fatal flaws of liberal democracy (McNeill et al., 1982). Its modern analogues—spanning Russia, China, and Iran—issue white papers, host conferences, and produce data designed to delegitimize the liberal order. They mimic the aesthetics of academia—ISSNs, editorial boards, peer-review façades—but begin with answers, not questions (Cia, 1955).

  • In Russia, state-affiliated think tanks within the Russian Academy of Sciences publish reports that recast repression as national security and democracy assistance as subversion. Their authors hold formal doctorates and security credentials, giving political messaging an aura of analytic depth (CIA, 1975)

  • China’s think tanks, such as the Chinese Academy of Social Sciences (CASS), propagate slogans like “democracy that works” and “Community of Common Destiny for Mankind,” framing one-party rule as modern governance (Chen, 2021; Sleeboom-Faulkner, 2007). Iran’s Islamic Propaganda Organization employs similar techniques, producing “academic” reports on Western moral decline that later reappear in state-aligned media as independent research.

Both Russia’s warfare concept and China’s “discourse power” theory identify pseudo-academic justification as a core element of information warfare. The KGB’s Operation Denver, which fabricated scientific “evidence” that the Pentagon created HIV/AIDS, was a prototype (Kramer, 2020; Selvage, 2019, 2021). The same pattern persisted in efforts to dispute chemical attacks in Syria (Bellingcat Investigation Team, 2018; Whitaker, 2021).

Once published, such “research” enters a feedback loop. State media cite it as third-party validation; officials quote the media; international commentators then repeat both, constructing a synthetic consensus. Authors are rewarded with promotion, visibility, and protection (Kovalik et al., 2024; RTUKnews, 2017; vanessabeeley, 2017; VanessaBeeley, 2018). When reprinted abroad—sometimes stripped of attribution—their falsehoods circulate as legitimate references (Bellingcat Investigation Team, 2018; Whitaker, 2021).

This is not research—it is the weaponization of its appearance. By simulating epistemic authority, malign academic initiatives turn credibility into a strategic asset.
