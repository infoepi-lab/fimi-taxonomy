Spillover

function
Spillover describes the diffusion of regime-favored narratives into public discourse through mechanisms that do not rely on explicit coordination, direct state control, or coercive amplification.
Author
Affiliation

E. Rosalie Li

Information Epidemiology Lab

Published

February 24, 2026

Spillover describes the diffusion of regime-favored narratives into public discourse through mechanisms that do not rely on explicit coordination, direct state control, or coercive amplification. Unlike traditional propaganda or high-intensity information manipulation campaigns, spillover mechanisms operate in the background, often imperceptibly, embedding manipulated or ideologically loaded narratives into the epistemic routines of everyday media consumption. Their power lies in subtlety: they exploit systemic vulnerabilities in how information is sourced, circulated, and socially validated to extend the reach and legitimacy of regime interests without triggering the defensive reflexes typically associated with overt state messaging.

Spillover functions through multiple discrete but overlapping techniques. Each leverages different facets of the modern information ecosystem—from attribution ambiguity to infrastructural dependency—to enable authoritarian narratives to appear organic, credible, and widely shared.

Mechanisms

Spillover operates through mechanisms that embed regime narratives into everyday information flows without overt coordination. Browse the mechanisms below:

Title Description
Algorithmic Propagation Algorithmic propagation amplifies misleading or sensational content on platforms like YouTube, TikTok, and X by prioritizing engagement metrics over credibility, leading to widespread exposure of disinformation through automated ranking systems.
Background Noise Persistent low-level repetition of regime-aligned messages normalizes falsehoods and diminishes audience skepticism through ambient familiarity.
Information Decay Information decay refers to the gradual loss of shared understanding and trust in facts due to ongoing exposure to conflicting narratives and diminished institutional credibility, leading to fragmented communities unable to reach a common consensus.
Organic Alignment Organic alignment occurs when individuals or groups independently adopt narratives that align with a regime’s goals, driven by shared ideology, grievances, or opportunism, thereby enhancing the regime’s narrative credibility.
Semantic Contagion Semantic contagion refers to the organic migration of specific linguistic or conceptual frames into different ideological domains, illustrating memetic spread without direct coordination.
Unwitting Engagement Individuals unknowingly enhance the credibility of manipulated content through genuine social interactions, obscuring its true origin and intent.

Discussion

Spillover represents one of the regime’s most insidious and structurally effective tools of narrative diffusion. It avoids overt confrontation, instead operating through saturation, ambiguity, and integration into the ambient rhythms of daily communication. By embedding manipulated content within the routine operations of journalism, social media, and civic discourse, spillover allows regime-aligned narratives to circulate widely, appear organic, and resist direct refutation.

Adding noise—the repeated, low-friction recirculation of ideas—adds atmospheric density, creating the illusion of consensus through sheer presence. Together, these mechanisms dissolve the distinction between independent thought and managed perception, subtly reshaping the informational environment while remaining difficult to trace.
