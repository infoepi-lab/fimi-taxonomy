FIMI Taxonomy v4.1 — Modular taxonomy of foreign information manipulation and interference

Sabotage

function
Sabotage encompasses deliberate actions designed to destabilize, fragment, or degrade adversaries through destructive rather than persuasive means.
Author
Affiliation

E. Rosalie Li

Information Epidemiology Lab

Published

February 24, 2026

Sabotage refers to the deliberate degradation of a target society’s internal cohesion, decision-making capacity, or epistemic stability through indirect, non-kinetic means. It is not necessarily destructive in the traditional sense, but corrosive—eroding the connective tissue of a polity from within. While it may appear incidental or decentralized, sabotage functions as a strategic weapon in the broader architecture of hybrid warfare, using information, psychology, and institutional vulnerabilities to destabilize from the inside out. This section outlines six core mechanisms through which sabotage operates as an instrument of state influence.

Mechanisms

Sabotage operates through multiple mechanisms that degrade institutional capacity, fragment social cohesion, and corrode epistemic stability. Browse the mechanisms below:

Title Description
Cognitive Attrition Cognitive attrition is a strategy that gradually undermines a population’s critical thinking, emotional resilience, and engagement with complex information, leading to apathy and withdrawal from public discourse through sustained exposure to conflicting claims and uncertainty.
Covert Criminal Activity Covert criminal activity involves the strategic use of illicit actors and networks by a state to achieve political objectives while maintaining plausible deniability and appearing as independent criminal acts.
Destructive Cyber Operations Destructive cyber operations involve covert or overt actions to compromise, disrupt, or exploit foreign information systems, including intrusions and sabotage, aimed at degrading institutional capacity and undermining public confidence.
Document and Archive Manipulation Document and archive manipulation targets the evidentiary foundations of truth through fabrication, alteration, suppression, or selective presentation of official documents, historical records, or archival material.
Elevate Extremists Elevate Extremists is a sabotage tactic that strengthens social divisions and weakens institutions by providing support to extremist groups, amplifying their influence, and undermining moderate voices.
Foment Violence Foment Violence refers to the strategic provocation and normalization of violence by external actors in a target society, using various methods to incite domestic actors to engage in violence, thereby destabilizing social cohesion and legitimizing further intervention.
Information Fracturing Information fracturing is the deliberate dissemination of multiple, conflicting narratives into the information environment to disorient audiences and degrade their ability to distinguish truth from falsehood.
Information Pollution Information pollution is a sabotage tactic that overwhelms governance systems through procedural flooding, humanitarian strain, and response saturation, forcing institutions to prioritize volume over merit and leading to their degradation, particularly in democracies.
Infrastructure Targeting Infrastructure targeting involves intentional actions to disrupt essential systems for society, leading to instability and loss of public confidence through various means such as attacks and cyber intrusions.
Irregular Attacks Irregular attacks are deliberate acts of physical sabotage aimed at damaging infrastructure and intimidating populations, differing from cyber operations by causing material harm and visible disruption without typically being classified as acts of war.
Malicious Software Malicious software (malware) is intentionally created to infiltrate and disrupt digital systems, enabling espionage, data theft, and sabotage while undermining operational continuity and public trust.
Stoking Intergroup Conflict Stoking intergroup conflict involves amplifying or fabricating grievances between groups to foster hostility and mistrust, exploiting perceived injustices to fragment social cohesion and destabilize governance.
Terrorism Terrorism involves non-state actors using violence and propaganda for political or ideological goals, often with state actors manipulating these groups to destabilize adversaries while maintaining plausible deniability.

Discussion

Sabotage, in the context of influence operations, is not episodic. It is systemic, recursive, and designed to become self-reinforcing. By embedding friction, confusion, and mistrust into the target society, it ensures that governance becomes harder, trust more fragile, and reform less likely. It is the corrosion of adversary capacity through ambient destabilization, accomplished not with bombs or bullets, but with narratives, networks, and institutional infection. This is sabotage as doctrine—not disruption for its own sake, but as preparation of the terrain for deeper influence or eventual intervention.
