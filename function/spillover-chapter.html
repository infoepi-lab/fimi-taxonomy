Spillover — Chapter

chapter
In-depth analysis of the Spillover function with citations
Author

Spillover describes the diffusion of regime-favored narratives into public discourse through mechanisms that do not rely on explicit coordination, direct state control, or coercive amplification. Unlike traditional propaganda or high-intensity information manipulation campaigns, spillover mechanisms operate in the background, often imperceptibly, embedding manipulated or ideologically loaded narratives into the epistemic routines of everyday media consumption. Their power lies in subtlety: they exploit systemic vulnerabilities in how information is sourced, circulated, and socially validated to extend the reach and legitimacy of regime interests without triggering the defensive reflexes typically associated with overt state messaging.

Spillover functions through multiple discrete but overlapping techniques. Each leverages different facets of the modern information ecosystem—from attribution ambiguity to infrastructural dependency—to enable authoritarian narratives to appear organic, credible, and widely shared.

Varieties

Incidental Alignment

Incidental alignment occurs when individuals or groups independently adopt positions or narratives that align with a regime’s interests, despite no formal or traceable interaction with the regime itself. This may emerge from ideological affinity, anti-establishment sentiment, shared geopolitical outlooks, or pragmatic opportunism. The regime benefits from the downstream effects of this alignment: the organic adoption of its talking points by seemingly unaffiliated actors can simulate spontaneous consensus, enabling manipulated narratives to enter mainstream discourse under the appearance of independent origination. This information manipulation leverages convergence rather than command, turning ideological proximity into narrative force projection.

Infrequent Citations

In this mechanism, regime-linked or state-affiliated content is cited by credible or high-profile outlets without adequate context or disclosure of the source’s affiliations. Even when citation is sporadic, the authority and reach of the citing entity confer a veneer of legitimacy on the underlying material. This process strategically blurs the distinction between vetted journalism and manipulated content. It embeds regime narratives into the architecture of public knowledge by smuggling them in through otherwise trustworthy channels. The outcome is not just expanded reach, but normalized presence—the perception that these ideas are part of the regular information diet of global discourse.

One-Way Referencing

One-way referencing denotes the asymmetrical replication of regime-originated content by external actors who do not receive reciprocal engagement. This flow creates a facade of independent endorsement while obscuring the origin of the message. The regime’s content is often adopted or echoed in foreign media, think tanks, or public forums, stripped of its original context or attribution. This tactic extends the narrative’s geographic and ideological range while preserving plausible deniability. It turns peripheral uptake into a tool of central influence, co-opting the credibility of foreign actors to launder the regime’s messaging.

Unattributed Quotes

Quoting without attribution is repeating regime-originated claims, tropes, or talking points without identifying their source. This technique fosters the illusion of independent validation while allowing regime narratives to bypass critical scrutiny. When these claims circulate as unattributed fragments—memes, slogans, opinion excerpts—they become part of the ambient informational terrain. The obscured authorship conceals intent and severs the message from its strategic context, allowing propaganda to masquerade as grassroots sentiment or conventional wisdom. This is especially potent when such unattributed material is deployed by influencers or commentators presumed to be ideologically neutral or disconnected from foreign state interests.

Routine Wire Service

Newsrooms worldwide, particularly those with limited resources or international reach, heavily rely on wire services to populate their coverage. Regimes exploit this dependency by inserting manipulated or ideologically charged content into syndicated news streams, either directly through state-sponsored outlets or indirectly through intermediaries. Once picked up, these stories are republished verbatim or with minimal editing across hundreds of secondary platforms, often without acknowledgment of origin. The replication of wire content gives manipulated narratives structural legitimacy—what appears as widespread reporting is, in fact, a broadcast from a single, orchestrated source. The credibility of the medium conceals the manipulation of the message.

Unwitting Engagement

Unwitting engagement occurs when individuals amplify regime content or narratives without understanding their ideological or strategic origins. This may take the form of social media shares, retweets, or discussions initiated by users who view the material as topical, amusing, or provocative, rather than as part of an influence campaign. The result is a form of authentic-seeming engagement that lends credibility and reach to otherwise manipulative content. By mobilizing genuine users to disseminate regime-favored perspectives, unwitting engagement transforms propaganda into participatory media. It aligns with the aesthetics and affective dynamics of grassroots activities, reducing friction for broader diffusion.

Noise

Noise refers to regime-aligned ideas, claims, and framings that persist at a persistent, low-intensity repetition across diverse information environments. Unlike overt persuasion or argumentation, noise operates through ambient saturation, a strategy of background reinforcement where repetition, rather than coherence or credibility, becomes the mechanism of influence. This phenomenon is based on the psychological principle of the “illusory truth effect,” where repeated exposure increases perceived truthfulness, regardless of the message’s factual accuracy or source credibility (Gordon Pennycook, 2017; Jiang Y, 2024; Pennycook G, 2017; Prooijen et al., 2018).

Noise is propagated through a dense and decentralized mesh of vectors: automated bot networks seeding replies on social media; partisan pundits echoing similar phrases across media appearances; ideologically aligned influencers resharing content in overlapping digital subcultures; and constant reappearances of particular claims in forums, comment sections, memes, and chat groups. Importantly, these instances rarely build a robust or logical case. Instead, they leverage frequency and visibility to build familiarity.

The strategic advantage of noise lies in its plausibility due to its ubiquity. When a narrative surfaces repeatedly in diverse, even marginal, contexts, it can create the illusion of consensus and normalize otherwise fringe positions. It also exploits cognitive shortcuts—audiences begin to accept a claim not because it has been substantiated, but because it feels familiar (Fazio LK, 2015; Hassan A, 2021).

Noise serves as a mechanism of cumulative conditioning. It does not require focused attention or critical engagement, but instead accrues through peripheral perception. In high-volume information environments, where cognitive fatigue and information overload are common, repeated exposure allows noise to displace more reasoned or verified information simply by crowding the field.

By flooding the discursive landscape with consistent messaging, noise ensures regime-favored narratives become default cognitive fixtures. It is not designed to convert but to erode skepticism, blunt critical faculties, and ensure that falsehoods are never far from recall. In this sense, noise operates as the soft tissue of information manipulation: ever-present, underacknowledged, and structurally integral to the architecture of spillover influence.

Discussion

Spillover represents one of the regime’s most insidious and structurally effective tools of narrative diffusion. It avoids overt confrontation, instead operating through saturation, ambiguity, and integration into the ambient rhythms of daily communication. By embedding manipulated content within the routine operations of journalism, social media, and civic discourse, spillover allows regime-aligned narratives to circulate widely, appear organic, and resist direct refutation.

Adding noise—the repeated, low-friction recirculation of ideas—adds atmospheric density, creating the illusion of consensus through sheer presence. Together, these mechanisms dissolve the distinction between independent thought and managed perception, subtly reshaping the informational environment while remaining difficult to trace.
